Everyone sells the same product — why does my margin keep shrinking?

2026-08-19

Hello from MAKENOV. What protects a distribution margin is not sales skill but product scarcity. A product anyone can source ends up competing on price alone, and nobody wins that fight.

Why do common products end in a price war?

When five sellers carry the same product, the only thing buyers compare is price. Same quality, same specs — no other criterion exists. One seller cuts 5%, the rest follow or lose the order. After a few rounds the margin you first calculated is gone: sales grow, profit does not.

This mechanism works regardless of the distributor\u2019s ability. Sell well, open more stores — with the same product you end up in the same place.

What makes an innovative product different?

With no substitute, comparison moves away from price. Buyers ask "where can I get this" before "how much". That creates three things: you can defend the price; exclusivity becomes an asset instead of an empty word; and retailers come to you, because there is only one channel.

What counts as truly innovative?

CriterionHow to verify
Core technologyPatent number and registration countries — filing and registration differ
Performance evidenceReports from accredited test labs, not only in-house data
SubstitutabilityWhether a same-function product already exists locally
Supply structureDirect from the manufacturer or through middlemen
Room for exclusivityWhether a Vietnam master distributor is already appointed

You do not need all five, but core technology and substitutability are mandatory checks — they decide your price defense.

What are the risks?

A product new to the market needs explaining, and that cost falls on the distributor. Early sales can be slow, and licensing may take longer. So ask for two things together: a sufficiently long exclusivity period and initial marketing support from the supplier, so you do not carry the market-opening cost alone.

Where to start?

Before looking at any product, ask: has it already entered Vietnam, and is the master distributorship already taken? If not taken, that is exactly when terms are most negotiable.

FAQ

Does innovative always mean good margin?
No. You need both no substitute and real demand; with only one of the two, it becomes inventory.

Is a patent enough?
A patent proves technology, not sales. Check whether Vietnam is among the registered countries.

Should I skip products already in distribution?
For exclusivity they rank low, but for adding items to an existing network they can still make sense.
